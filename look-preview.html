What you're looking at, and what it would take

The layout is not the problem

Every panel above sits at the exact percentage it sits at in css/style.css. Nothing moved. The whole difference between "Today" and A/B/C is material: surface, light, palette, type, texture. That matters because it means none of this touches recomputeGridMetrics, the 747×420 stage, or a single JS file.

The three things doing the damage

1. Value is constant, hue varies. Measured against the background, every current accent sits between 6.8:1 and 10.4:1 — mint 10.4, cyan 9.6, violet 8.4, coral 6.9. Six saturated hues all shouting at one volume. That's why the screen reads busy and flat at the same time. Hit the greyscale test above: Today's hierarchy disappears completely.

2. The borders aren't reaching the eye. --border #3a3020 is 1.51:1 against the background; #46381f is 1.72:1. WCAG asks 3:1 for a component boundary. Enclosure is the strongest grouping tool there is and it's currently below threshold, so nine panels read as one undifferentiated field.

3. The cards float on nothing. There is no table, no texture, no light source, and no shadow anywhere on screen. A card game without a surface reads as unfinished no matter how good the cards are — and the cards are good.

Two palettes, not one

A card face is #F4EAD5, luminance 0.83. Measured on it: amber 1.53:1, cyan 1.28:1, phosphor green 1.36:1. No neon accent can be drawn on a card. So the game needs a dark-field palette with luminous accents for the HUD, and a separate card-ink palette of dark accents for anything drawn on a card face. cardBandInk already discovered half of this empirically in r302; naming it stops it recurring.

The same measurement disqualifies an all-light UI: a cream card on pale paper is 1.14:1 and vanishes. That's why B keeps its board dark.

Why the CRT reads weak — it's diagnosable

#cab-screen::after is inside #cabinet, which carries zoom: ~1.92. A 1px line on a 3px period paints at 1.92px on a 5.77px period — fractional on both, so consecutive lines land in different sub-pixel phase and moiré. This is the same bug r300/r302 already solved on the card corner bands.

Then: mix-blend-mode: overlay pivots at 50% luminance, so it darkens dark panels ~14% and cream cards ~2.9% — backwards, since a real tube's structure is most visible in midtones. Net modulation is about 2%. A real CRT is 25–45%.

And three of the four cues are simply absent: no phosphor triad (scanlines alone read as blinds over a screenshot), no bloom (bright areas must visibly eat the scanline gaps — this is the cue that sells emissive phosphor), no geometry.

Direction B has the fixed version, confined to the board where a screen actually is. Integer 4px pitch, multiply, soft ramp with a plateau, 3px RGB triad, and a bloom pass. Drag the CRT slider.

The technical path

This is the good news. css/style.css is 316KB, but all nine landscape HUD panels get their material from one rule — the shared chrome block that sets background: var(--panel); border: 1px solid var(--border). Four more declarations carry the rest: body, #grid, .card, and the action buttons.

So the shape is a theme layer: one stylesheet loaded after style.css that overrides surfaces and leaves geometry alone. The codebase already works this way — the LETHE cabinet reskin at line ~5100 is exactly that pattern, just living inside the big file instead of its own.

Honest caveat: ~623 hardcoded hex literals sit outside the token system, mostly in particles, entity tiles and FX. A theme layer reaches the surfaces on day one; the accents get swept afterwards, file by file, and that sweep is worth doing anyway — four token names (--cream, --gold, --border, --cream-dim) currently hold two different values each depending on which rule wins.

Grit is a dial, not a destination

The PS2 look isn't a different art direction — it's the same one degraded along a time axis: grain 0.03 → 0.10 and fine → chunky, neutrals desaturating, accents narrowing from three to one, bevel highlights flattening, vignette deepening, edge wear appearing. Drag the grit slider. Whichever direction you pick, it ages down that curve without changing dialect.
